# Humanizer editing record

The report uses the Humanizer skill's draft, audit and revision process. The technical register stays neutral; casual insults or theatrical certainty would make the scientific review worse.

## Draft audit

- Early tables exposed internal scenario codes. Replace those with month, operating assumptions and location in ordinary language.
- A controller catalog is not a recommendation. State which policy passed the relevant matched comparison and where it failed.
- A precise fleet count can suggest unsupported real-world precision. Use "passing tested fleet" and explain the next failed observation and the separate energy upper bound.
- Repeated warnings about synthetic data become boilerplate if they do not explain a consequence. Tie each limitation to the affected claim: winter certification, LV assets, charging location or search optimality.
- Define net node demand separately from gross supply so readers can reproduce the formula.
- Remove em dashes, en dashes, promotional wording and generic conclusions. Preserve numerical details, unsuccessful cases and reviewer findings.

## Revision

The final report answers the five questions directly, uses plain scenario names, gives service-gated comparisons, identifies boundary failure causes, and separates unsafe-baseline intervals visually. It preserves the supplied monthly discrepancy rather than smoothing it away. The draft and final text files accompany the PDF; subsequent reviewer and layout corrections are reflected in the final text.
